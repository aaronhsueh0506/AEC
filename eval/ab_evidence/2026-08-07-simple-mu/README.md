### What the previous version of this file got wrong

It said *"Worst Δdeg anywhere is −0.034"*. **−0.034 is a bucket mean.** The same
JSONs contain case-level Δdeg of −0.349, −0.282, −0.232 and −0.207. Calling a
mean "anywhere" turned an average into a claim about every case, and it is the
one claim a reviewer would use to decide the change is safe.

It also said *"No other bucket moves"*, which is true of bucket means and false
of cases: `FS_movement` (n=25) hides Δecho of −0.162, −0.152 and −0.115, and
`DT_movement` (n=26) hides the four worst Δdeg values in the whole run.

Bucket means are close to neutral almost by construction when a change helps
some clips and hurts others — so a report built on means cannot fail in the way
that matters. That is why the numbers above are now **generated**, per case, by
a tool, and why `ab_report.py` prints mean, median, p10, the worst five, and the
counts below each threshold for every bucket. A hand-written summary got one
free pass; it does not get a second.

## Investigation

`eval/simple_mu_case_probe.py` runs one clip through the driver's own
`run_ours()` twice — all-frozen and all-retimed — and records the full simple-mu
state trajectory plus target-free physical measures.

### What this corpus can and cannot support

`wav/aec_challenge_blind` ships **mic and lpb only. There is no clean near-end
in any scenario** — not even `nearend_singletalk`, whose lpb sits ~3.7 dB below
its mic, i.e. the far end is playing there too. **SI-SDR and STOI are therefore
not computable on this corpus**; any figure presented as one would be scored
against a reference that still contains the echo the AEC is meant to remove. The
DT and NE degradations here cannot be physically decoupled, and the honest
options are the target-free split below plus a listening test — the latter is a
human step and has not been done.

The target-free split, which needs no reference:

| | meaning |
|---|---|
| `d_near_db` | Output change on **far-inactive** hops. No echo is arriving, so everything in the mic is near end. Negative = the candidate removed near end the baseline kept. Unambiguous. |
| `d_far_db` | Output change on **far-active** hops. On an FS clip the output *is* residual echo, so positive = worse. On a DT clip the two are superimposed and this number alone cannot separate them. |

Far activity comes from the lpb, delay-compensated by the run's own
cross-correlation — used only to place the mask, never fed to the AEC.

### First result

`HAxmF7v4dE0itSp5R5B3Dw_farend_singletalk`, 16k/256, the worst Δecho in the
Python run:

```
first divergence   hop 53 (424 ms) -- during convergence, not at a late event
d_far_db           +0.45   more residual echo while the far end is active
d_near_db          −3.27
branches  base     onset 126, ongoing 434, hold 1588, release 660
          cand     onset 100, ongoing 439, hold 1668, release 601
```

The mechanism is legible: at this grid 200 ms is **25 hops against the frozen
20**, so each arming holds mu frozen longer. The candidate takes 26 fewer fresh
onsets and 80 more hold hops, adapts less during far-end activity, and leaves
more residual echo. That is the retiming doing exactly what it was specified to
do — and the cost is visible on this clip.

This is the point the state machine raises: **converting each alpha and the
holdoff correctly does not make the state machine wall-clock invariant.** The
branch condition is re-evaluated every hop, the counter only decrements on the
hold branch, and the holdoff length feeds back into the ratio trajectory that
selects the branch. Whether the invariant that should be preserved is "200 ms of
hold" or something else — the fraction of time frozen, the adaptation deficit
per second — is an open design question this A/B did not answer.

Remaining probes over all 16 flagged stems at both grids are in
`probes/` (in progress).

## Sample-size limits found while widening the cohort

A ≥25-case static far-end cohort was requested. **It cannot be built from the
data present**: `wav/aec_challenge_blind/farend_singletalk` contains exactly
**5** static stems, and all 5 are already in the 90-case manifest. `doubletalk`
has **4** static stems. The corpus is overwhelmingly `_with_movement`.

The full 800-case AEC Challenge corpus is **not on disk** (`wav/aec_challenge/`
does not exist; only the blind subset is present). So:

* the FS_static bucket cannot be made statistically robust here, and its n=5
  mean should not be used as a gate in either direction;
* the release-grade 800-case run cannot be performed on this machine, and no
  verdict recorded here substitutes for it;
* fetching the full corpus is a prerequisite for any ship decision and is a
  separate, authorised step.

What *can* be strengthened without more data is the per-case criterion, which is
what the report above now applies to all 90 cases and 30 FS / 30 DT cases rather
than to a 5-case bucket.

## Acceptance criteria — all must hold

`c4b7382` (and any successor) stays NO-GO until every one of these is met:

1. every bucket mean inside the ±0.05 band, in **both** ports and **both**
   16 kHz grids;
2. no unexplained audible regression in the worst-N per case;
3. every case with Δecho ≤ −0.1 or Δdeg ≤ −0.1 resolved by physical measurement
   or listening — a bucket mean does not resolve one;
4. a root cause for the Python/C sign disagreement on the same clip, not merely
   the observation that both ports diverge there;
5. both 16 kHz grids pass, and 48 kHz has **native 48 kHz** release evidence,
   not a structural pass;
6. the release-grade 800-case run, on the full corpus.

Failing any of these, the correct outcome is to **freeze the whole simple-mu
batch** and document it as a known grid-specific exception — not to keep a
partial retime. The four constants are one mechanism; freezing three and
retiming one is measurably worse than either extreme (see the bisect below).

Each constant alone against the five FS_static clips (`fs_static_bisect.txt`):

On the worst clip each of holdoff / hold / release costs ~+1.2 dB **alone**
against +0.44 for all four. Splitting the batch to chase one bucket makes the
result worse, not smaller.

Six points cannot separate an interaction from an accident, though: four binary
knobs have 16 settings and only the two extremes plus four interior points were
measured. `eval/simple_mu_interaction_matrix.py` runs all 16 — its purpose is
not to pick a winner but to test whether the state machine is wall-clock
invariant at all, and whether a *common* anchor for the four beats the current
per-constant conversion.

## What is established and not in dispute

* The corpus does exercise the mechanism. The branch census
  (`branch_census_g{256,512}.json`) covers all 90 cases; every branch fires
  heavily at both grids (16k/256: 9925 fresh onsets, 83095 ongoing, 121377
  hold, 166923 release over 381320 hops). A neutral result on an unreached
  branch would prove nothing about its constant; that escape is closed.
* It is not a no-op: 83/90 and 82/90 cases changed at the two C grids
  (`c_wav_comparison_g{256,512}.json`), worst |diff| 0.71 and 1.15 LSB.
* 200 ms → 12 hops on a 16 ms grid is deliberate ties-to-even, and the two
  ports agree: C `lrintf()` and Python `round()` are both round-half-even.
* The four constants are one mechanism and must move together or not at all.
