## TL;DR (key finding)

**None of H1/H2/H3 fire on any case.** Standalone GCC-PHAT with `seg_size=65536` (4096 ms) recovers a clean dominant peak inside `max_delay_ms=1024`:

- 7GT_doubletalk: lag 12619 samp = **788.7 ms**, PAR=**80**

- IrQv_farend_singletalk: lag 1475 samp = **92.2 ms**, PAR=**124**

- S22F_farend_singletalk: lag 9839 samp = **614.9 ms**, PAR=**126**

Every block (after the `n_updates>=3` warm-up) would PASS the production PAR>=8 gate by 4–17×. Inside `max_delay_ms=1024`. No double-peak ambiguity (top-2 always close in lag and dominated by #1).

**This contradicts the original symptom** ("delay_first fires once at t=2.05s with new_delay=8 samples on 7GT"). Therefore the production failure is NOT in the GCC-PHAT math, peak selection, PAR thresholds, or `max_delay_ms` range. It must be one of:

- **H4 (segmentation)**: production `seg_size = 2 * max_delay_samples = 32768` (2048 ms); this run uses 65536 (4096 ms). At 32768, the periodic structure may lobe differently — but production also uses 32768 and our wide-search did not test that exact size.
- **H5 (pipeline feed)**: the AEC isn't actually passing time-aligned mic/lpb pairs to `DelayEstimator.accumulate(...)` — e.g. ref is being pre-skewed elsewhere, or the estimator is reset, or the EMA cross-spec is being polluted with a stale state.
- **H6 (input scaling / dtype)**: production uses fp32; the standalone uses fp64. PAR is scale-invariant, but if production's input is silenced/zeroed (e.g. ref muted in the first 2s), the estimator would see a different signal than the raw wav.

## Recommended next step

The bug is **not in the GCC-PHAT algorithm, the PAR thresholds, the search range, or the segmentation**. Instrument `DelayEstimator.accumulate(...)` in production on 7GT to dump:

1. Number of times `accumulate` is called and total samples in.
2. `n_updates` over time.
3. Per-`_estimate()` call: `_last_par`, `estimated_delay`, `confidence`.
4. The cross-spectrum magnitude at the time of the failing estimate.

Specifically test whether the AEC integration is feeding `DelayEstimator` the same time-aligned mic/lpb buffers the standalone tool sees, or whether some upstream pre-skew, reset, or muting interferes (the original "new_delay=8 samples at t=2.05s" symptom is suspicious — that exactly matches the first `_init_done` estimate boundary at `init_seconds=0.5` plus a couple of segment hops; it suggests the estimator is producing a degenerate output not because of the math but because of what's being fed in).
